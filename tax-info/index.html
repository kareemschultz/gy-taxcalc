The 2026 rules behind GYCalc's calculators, one plain answer per topic. GYCalc is independent of the GRA and NIS.

PAYE (income tax)

25% on up to GY$280,000 a month, 35% on the rest.

Calculate your take-home pay

PAYE (Pay As You Earn) is income tax your employer takes from each pay and sends to the GRA. For each pay period, GYCalc works it out like this:

  1. Add up your gross pay: basic pay, allowances, overtime and second-job income.
  2. Work out NIS: 5.6% of gross pay, up to the earnings ceiling.
  3. Take out non-taxable allowances and the tax-free part of overtime and second-job income. What is left is your balance of income.
  4. Take off the personal allowance: the fixed amount for your pay period, or a third of your balance of income if that is more.
  5. Also take off NIS, any child allowances and the insurance deduction. The rest is chargeable income (never below zero).
  6. Charge 25% on chargeable income up to the band limit and 35% on anything above it.
  7. Your take-home pay is gross pay minus NIS, PAYE, insurance and any loan or credit-union deductions.

Personal allowance

The first GY$140,000 a month is tax-free, or a third of your pay if that is more.

On monthly pay, a third is the larger amount once your balance of income is above GY$420,000. Below that, the fixed figure applies. The fixed figure for each pay period is in the PAYE table above.

GRA's 2026 notice puts it this way: "GY$140,000, or one-third of Balance of Income after allowing for Second Job and Overtime statutory allowance, whichever is greater." GYCalc follows GRA's payroll reading: the third is taken of your pay less non-taxable allowances and the tax-free overtime and second-job amounts.

The law is worded differently. Section 20(1)(a) of the Income Tax Act (as replaced by the Income Tax (Amendment) Act No. 2 of 2025, and changed by the Fiscal Enactments (Amendment) Act 2026, Act No. 3 of 2026, which GRA calls the Income Tax (Amendment) Act), sets the fixed amount at GY$1,680,000 a year (GY$140,000 × 12) and keeps "one third of an individual's total income from all sources excluding income subject to withholding taxes under section 39(1)". It does not mention taking overtime or second-job amounts off first. The 2026 Act changed only the fixed amount.

So GRA's payroll reading and the statute's wording differ. It only matters when you have tax-free overtime or second-job pay and your pay is near or above GY$420,000 a month; GRA's reading then gives more tax. Needs verification: Official evidence is missing or contradictory.

NIS contributions

5.6% of your pay, up to GY$280,000 a month (at most GY$15,680).

NIS comes off before PAYE is worked out, so it also lowers your tax. This covers the employee's share only.

NIS publishes the monthly and weekly ceilings, and GRA's 2026 notice gives the yearly one. GRA caps the fortnightly contribution at 7,237 and labels a worked-example column with the ceiling behind it, without stating it as a rule; neither states a daily ceiling. So GYCalc derives the fortnightly ceiling from that worked example and the daily one from the yearly ceiling divided by 365, and labels any result that depends on them as derived.

What counts for NIS

NIS is charged on gross earnings received in cash. The NIS Collection of Contributions Regulations (regulation 17(2)) say this includes:

  • overtime payments
  • cost-of-living bonus
  • family allowances
  • commission or profits on sales
  • payments for night or shift work
  • holiday pay, and amounts set aside through the year to be paid out periodically

The word "including" means this list is not complete. The same regulation says Christmas bonuses, annual production bonuses, severance payments and terminal gratuities are not counted. It has no tax-style exemption for overtime, so GYCalc charges NIS on all overtime (a reading of the text, not an NIS statement) Derived: Calculated from authoritative published values..

Other allowances: unconfirmed
Travel, subsistence, entertainment, telephone, security, meal, housing, station and uniform allowances are not named in the regulation. Whether each counts as earnings or only repays a cost is unconfirmed. GYCalc charges NIS on all of your gross pay, including non-taxable allowances, overtime and second-job income. If your payslip differs, ask your employer or NIS.

Source: NIS Collection of Contributions Regulations, regulation 17(2), in the L.R.O. 1/2012 revision that NIS publishes. Later amendments were not ruled out.

Taxable and non-taxable pay

Basic pay and most allowances are taxed. A few allowances are tax-free only when they are used for the job.

GRA's list of taxable and non-taxable allowances decides. For many items it depends on what the allowance is for and how it is used, so an employer calling an allowance "non-taxable" isn't enough on its own.

Taxed: basic pay; duty, acting, risk and responsibility allowances; qualification allowances; overtime or second-job income above the tax-free amounts; transport from home to work; and the part of a housing allowance above the allowable portion (GRA doesn't say how much that is). GRA adds that allowances and benefits that are often disguised pay are taxable.

Tax-free only if used for its purpose (Income Tax Act s.5(b)(ii)): travelling for business, subsistence, entertainment, telephone, security, meal, station (interior, river or North West) and expense allowances. You must be able to show GRA it was spent that way, for example with receipts or vouchers. If it wasn't, or it was for personal use, it is taxed.

Uniform: GRA's list says a clothing allowance is "NOT considered taxable if wearing the item is a condition of employment, and it is not worn for general use". Both conditions apply.

Motor car travelling: GRA's list shows a "Motor Car Travelling Allowance" (for travelling extensively by motor car on official duties) among the taxable allowances, while a travelling allowance proved to be spent for business purposes is exempt. GRA doesn't reconcile the two. If you get a fixed car allowance with no receipts, ask GRA which applies.

GYCalc can't check how an allowance is used, so it uses the split you enter. Your payslip or employer tells you which of your allowances are non-taxable, and the tax-free part of overtime and second-job income is taken off as well.

Lower your tax: the personal allowance, NIS, child allowances and the insurance deduction come off before tax is charged. Loan and credit-union deductions come out of your take-home pay but do not lower your tax.

Overtime and second job

The first GY$50,000 a month of overtime, and of second-job income, is tax-free.

Each has its own limit; anything above it is taxed as normal pay.

Child allowance

GY$10,000 a month for each child under 18 comes off your taxable income.

Who can claim (GRA): one person per child. A "person" includes a mother, father, stepmother or stepfather, adoptive parent, guardian, or anyone who has treated the child as a child of the family. "Child" includes a step-child, an adopted child and a child of the family. The Income Tax Act (section 20(2), in Act No. 2 of 2025) adds that if more than one person is entitled, they choose one of them. You claim on GRA's "Claim for Deduction in Respect of Child Form" (the 2026 notice calls it the "Child Deduction Claim Form"); your employer can't give the deduction without it.

Not settled, so both versions are recorded:

  • Weekly, fortnightly and daily pay. GRA says the allowance is "apportioned according to the individual's earning period", but its FAQ says the Act apportions it monthly. GRA publishes no weekly, fortnightly or daily amount, so those figures in the table are GYCalc's, worked out from the yearly amount; they are not GRA's.
  • A child turning 18. The Income Tax Act (section 20(1)(b), in Act No. 2 of 2025) tests the child's age at the start of the year, so a child under 18 on 1 January counts for that year. GRA's FAQ says to pro-rate "up to the month" the child turns 18. GYCalc applies neither: you enter the number of children.
  • Living in Guyana. GRA's FAQ says the child must be living in Guyana. We did not find that condition in the Act's wording.

Medical and life insurance premiums

Medical and life insurance premiums you pay lower your tax, up to GY$50,000 a month.

The amount that counts is the smallest of: the premiums you actually pay, 10% of your gross salary, or the cap for your pay period. Medical and life insurance premiums both count.

Vacation allowance

A vacation allowance is tax-free up to one month's gross salary. Anything above that is taxed.

This is Income Tax Act s.5(b)(iiA), explained in GRA's vacation allowance policy. Since 1 January 2018 it covers private-sector as well as public-sector employees, and private-sector employees don't have to submit proof. Anything above one month's gross salary is added to your salary and taxed.

GRA does not say what "gross salary" includes (basic pay only, or also regular allowances), or over which pay periods the taxed excess is spread. NIS regulation 17(2) lists "holiday pay" as earnings but doesn't say whether a vacation allowance counts. GYCalc counts one month of basic pay plus regular allowances, without overtime or second-job pay; adds the excess to the pay period it is paid in; and charges no NIS on it. All three are assumptions. Assumption: A necessary modelling assumption because the source doesn't specify the treatment.

Gratuity

A lump sum built from a share of your basic pay each month, paid every few months. The Government contract rate is 22.5% of basic salary (Public Service Ministry Circular 3/2009).

See your gratuity months in Plan My Year

Government and public-service contracts follow a standard contract: gratuity of 22.5% of basic salary, calculated on completed 3-month periods of resident service and paid at 6-month intervals (the employee may choose to be paid every 6 months), subject to satisfactory performance. That is Public Service Ministry Circular 3/2009, with the same rate in the earlier Circulars 20/2000 and 2/2001 and in rule A8 of the Public Service Rules (1987 reprint). The Rules and the 2000 template count approved leave as service in a final lump sum (not vacation leave when paid every 6 months); the 2009 template says accrued leave does not count. The 2009 text is later, and GYCalc does not adjust for leave. GYCalc applies the rate to basic salary only, because the circular says "basic salary"; allowances are not in the base. That reading, and counting only completed 3-month periods, is derived from the wording, not confirmed by the Ministry.

No official rate was found for private-sector gratuity, or for statutory bodies and state-owned companies, whose rate may differ from the Government template (needs verification). For those, 22.5% is only a starting value: change it to the rate in your contract, and say when the contract started and how often it pays. Payouts come at the end of each cycle counted from that month.

Whether PAYE is taken depends on who the contract is with. GRA lists "gratuities paid in lieu of pensions to Government employees in respect of contracts of employment or service" as not taxed (GRA cites Income Tax Act s.13(z); the section is not yet confirmed against the Act text), and says private-sector gratuity is taxed. GYCalc taxes it with the pay of the pay period it is paid in, an assumption until GRA confirms the method, and takes no NIS from it. The NIS regulations leave out "terminal gratuities"; whether a gratuity paid while your contract continues counts is unconfirmed.

Importing a vehicle

Under 4 years old: , and . 4 years or older: excise only.

Estimate your import cost

Import taxes are worked out on the vehicle's CIF value: the price you paid (FOB) plus shipping and insurance to Guyana. Prices in US dollars are converted at an exchange rate. GYCalc uses GRA's customs rate, GY$208.50 per US$1, which is the rate customs uses; you can try another.

The tax depends on the vehicle's age (under 4 years, or 4 years and older), its engine size and its fuel. New hybrids (petrol, diesel and plug-in alike) up to 2,000cc pay duty only: no excise and no VAT, from GRA's 2026 table. Between 2,000cc and 2,500cc, GRA's table charges excise, but the hybrid wording in the Excise Tax Regulations can be read to keep the exemption Needs verification: Official evidence is missing or contradictory.An official-source conflict for new hybrids of 2,001-2,500cc. GRA's ASYCUDA table (PDF last modified 25 May 2026) lists "Other - Hybrid" at 45% duty, 110% excise and 14% VAT, the same on all four hybrid lines (8703.40.00.49, 8703.50.00.39, 8703.60.00.49, 8703.70.00.39). Excise Tax Regulations Table A-15 (revised 2025-03-10) says the exemption "does not apply to hybrid vehicles (a) exceeding four years old from the date of manufacture to the date of importation; and (b) with an engine capacity exceeding 2000cc". A new hybrid is not over four years old, so the regulation's wording, read literally, keeps the exemption. Table A-15 was not amended in 2026. GYCalc computes GRA's current ASYCUDA treatment (110% excise) first and shows the exempt reading as the other end of the range; confirm with GRA.. Budget 2026 changes

In force from 16 Feb 2026. Larger double-cabs (over 2,500cc) pay the standard rates; GRA doesn't say what applies to ones 4 years and older.

  • VAT removed on vehicles under 4 years old with engines of 1,500cc or less.
  • VAT removed on hybrid vehicles with engines of 2,000cc or less (GRA's notice: new hybrids under 4 years; older vehicles already pay no VAT).
  • Double-cab pickups, 2,000cc or less: one flat charge of GY$2,000,000 instead of duty, excise and VAT.
  • Double-cab pickups, 2,001–2,500cc: one flat charge of GY$3,000,000 instead of duty, excise and VAT.
  • ATVs only (not UTVs): no import taxes.
  • GYCalc also applies the VAT removal to vans and single-cab pickups. Glossary

Plain meanings of PAYE, NIS, CIF, FOB, principal and other terms.

PAYE (Pay As You Earn)
Income tax your employer takes from each pay and sends to the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA).
NIS (National Insurance Scheme)
Guyana's social insurance. Contributions from your pay go towards benefits such as sickness, maternity and old-age pensions.
Gross pay
Everything you earn in a pay period before anything is taken out: basic pay, allowances, overtime and second-job income.
Balance of income
Gross pay minus non-taxable allowances and the tax-free part of overtime and second-job income. The personal allowance is worked out from this.
Chargeable income
The part of your pay PAYE is charged on: balance of income minus the personal allowance, NIS, child allowances and the insurance deduction.
Take-home pay
What reaches your bank account: gross pay minus NIS, PAYE, insurance and any loan or credit-union deductions.
Gratuity
A lump sum some contracts pay on top of salary, built up as a percentage of basic pay each month.
FOB (free on board)
The price of a vehicle where it is bought, before shipping and insurance.
CIF (cost, insurance and freight)
FOB price plus shipping and insurance to Guyana. Import taxes are worked out on this value.
Import duty
A customs tax charged as a percentage of a vehicle's CIF value.
Excise tax
A tax on certain goods, including vehicles. For vehicles it depends on engine size and age.
VAT (value-added tax)
A tax on goods and services. On an imported vehicle it is 14% of CIF plus duty and excise, unless an exemption applies.
Re-migrant
A Guyanese returning to live in Guyana. Qualifying re-migrants pay a reduced tax on an imported vehicle.
Principal
The amount you borrow, before any interest.
Interest rate
What the lender charges for the loan, as a percentage of the balance per year.
Amortisation
Paying a loan off in regular instalments. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal.
